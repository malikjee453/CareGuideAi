TITLE: Diabetes
PUBLISHER: World Health Organization (WHO)

UPDATED: 2024-11-14

Diabetes is a chronic disease in which the body does not produce enough insulin or cannot effectively use the insulin it produces. Insulin regulates blood glucose.

Common symptoms can include increased thirst, frequent urination, tiredness, blurred vision, and unintentional weight loss. Symptoms can be mild, especially in type 2 diabetes, and diagnosis requires appropriate blood-glucose testing rather than symptoms alone.

Type 1 diabetes requires insulin for survival. Type 2 diabetes is the most common form and is associated with factors including excess weight, physical inactivity, and genetics.

Healthy eating, regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy body weight, and avoiding tobacco can help prevent or delay type 2 diabetes. Diabetes can be managed with lifestyle measures, medicines when needed, and monitoring for complications.

Diabetes can affect the heart, blood vessels, eyes, kidneys, nerves, and feet. Early diagnosis and appropriate ongoing care can help prevent or delay complications.
