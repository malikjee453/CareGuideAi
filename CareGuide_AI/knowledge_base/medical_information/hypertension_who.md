TITLE: Hypertension
PUBLISHER: World Health Organization (WHO)
URL: https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hypertension
CATEGORY: medical_information
UPDATED: 2025-09-25

Hypertension, also called high blood pressure, is persistently elevated pressure in the blood vessels. It is common and can be serious if untreated.

Many people with hypertension do not notice symptoms. Blood pressure measurement is therefore important for detection.

WHO describes hypertension as a blood pressure of 140/90 mmHg or higher when measured on two different days. Individual assessment and treatment decisions should be made by an appropriate healthcare professional.

Risk factors can include older age, genetics, overweight or obesity, physical inactivity, high-salt diets, tobacco use, and excessive alcohol consumption.

Lifestyle measures such as healthier eating, reducing salt, being physically active, maintaining a healthy weight, and stopping tobacco can help reduce risk. Some people require medicines prescribed by a healthcare professional.

Very high blood pressure can occur with symptoms such as severe headache, chest pain, difficulty breathing, confusion, or vision changes. When very high blood pressure occurs with concerning symptoms, immediate medical care is appropriate.
